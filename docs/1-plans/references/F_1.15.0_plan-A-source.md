# Capital-Planning Feasibility Workflow (F_1.15.0)

## Status and purpose

Revised planning draft for internal investment decision support. This replaces
the earlier attachment's proposed methodology and integration instructions; it
does not implement the feature or claim that a formal implementation review
passed.

Review baseline: origin/main at f01c9668a12684b5a1e191c26bbaeb1fb37a0ffc.
Re-verify source anchors before implementation. Governing policy is resolved
from protected origin/main:
docs/governance/solo-internal-change-and-production-policy.md.

Answer: given a supported capital budget, paced initial checks, conditional
graduation assumptions, and a user-selected follow-on policy, how many companies
can the fund support, where does expected capital go, and what fails under
specified stresses?

An optional aggregate performance case answers a separate informational
question: what might this holding receive at an entered exit value/date under
summarized preference terms? Its proceeds never enter construction capital,
follow-on funding, recycling or the feasibility verdict.

The attached report, VC Follow-On Reserve Platform: Operating Playbook and Model
Appendix, contributes selected ideas. Its production-specification label,
instructions, examples, and asserted standards are reference material. Current
owner instructions and repository policy determine scope. Prior Drive documents
remain optional references; the screenshot is illustrative only. The attached
Tactyc "Updating valuations" document is also illustrative. Its UI instructions
and conversion examples are not governing requirements or proof of exact
calculation behavior. User-entered valuations are scenario inputs, not valuation
recommendations.

## Product boundary

Two product-facing user classes map to the existing partner and analyst roles:

- Partner: evaluates alternatives and exercises existing partner capabilities.
- Analyst: prepares assumptions and scenarios and evaluates comparisons.

Reuse existing team-write permissions and fund access. Both classes may perform
scenario operations where currently authorized. Admin and service remain
existing technical identities; no new role hierarchy, investment approval
workflow, or RBAC migration is introduced. Legacy viewer maps to analyst; do not
assume that label implies read-only access. Grounding:
shared/auth/effective-roles.ts, server/lib/auth/jwt.ts, and
server/routes/fund-scenario-sets.ts.

In scope:

- Pure portfolio construction with fractional expected company counts.
- Allocation-specific sector/stage assumptions, explicit units and provenance.
- Fixed-dollar and pro-rata follow-on policies calculated with stage valuations,
  primary round sizes, option-pool dilution and retained ownership.
- Graduation eligibility, participation frequency and editable benchmark
  defaults.
- Cumulative monthly timing and annual presentation.
- Derived reserve ratios, designated follow-on earmarks and lifetime capital
  feasibility under a callable-as-needed assumption.
- Saved hypothetical scenario sets, persisted results, comparison and copy text.
- Deterministic named stresses, input-quality notices and shortfall diagnostics.
- Informational aggregate performance cases with user-entered exit equity value
  and date, ownership and summarized preference terms, approximate proceeds,
  MOIC, conversion/cap breakpoints, and manual ownership/FMV overrides.

Out of scope:

- Formal financial reporting, reporting-standard conformance, LP reporting,
  accounting certification, score-based investment recommendations, IC approval
  chains, valuation committees, and investor/auditor user classes.
- Credit or subscription facilities, borrowing capacity, interest, repayment,
  LP-default simulation, or financing an otherwise infeasible plan with
  borrowing.
- Current-liquidity execution, observed cash gates, capital-call receipt
  schedules, settlement constraints and cash-reserve transfer calendars. These
  belong in a separate operational forecast.
- Fund carry/net-return engines, automated valuation recommendations, Monte
  Carlo, percentiles, probability-of-shortfall claims, portfolio optimization
  and company ranking. Aggregate deterministic exit/proceeds cases are allowed
  as the informational companion defined below.
- Automatic reallocation, reduced checks, delayed rounds, or automatic
  recalculation/publication after source changes.
- Actual-portfolio integration, live data mutation, C1/C2/C3, operating-plan
  promotion, and authoritative actuals or reserves.
- Share-level SAFE/note conversion, warrant exercise, exact
  option/security-class or cap-table dilution, secondary transactions,
  legal-grade liquidation waterfalls, arbitrary target-ownership solvers and
  automatic policy optimization. Missing share-level precision does not exclude
  a complete aggregate case with explicit ownership and summarized terms.
- Using forecast exit proceeds as construction capital or modeled recycling.

Actual-mode terminology is settled for future integration: known investment and
financing dates replace modeled history; probabilities and lags apply only to
future unknown transitions. Historical graduations are not reweighted as
forecasts. This draft does not implement that future mode.

No new route, table, migration, queue, flag, or dependency is proposed. Package
version remains 1.6.0; F_1.15.0 is an initiative identifier.

## Accepted owner decisions

1. Complete supported fee and fund-expense inputs are mandatory, including when
   an explicit net investment budget is provided. An override never waives
   source validation or unsupported fee-basis refusal.
2. New companies enter regularly, uniformly over the selected deployment period.
   Each entry cohort follows the same allocation-specific round cadence.
3. Use monthsAfterPreviousRound, accumulated from the immediately preceding
   financing round. Month 0 plus 24 plus 18 produces months 24 and 42; an entry
   cohort at month 12 produces months 36 and 54.
4. Capital may be called as scheduled investments require it. F1.15 asks whether
   the fund can support the portfolio over its lifetime; it does not ask whether
   an investment can execute today. This final clarification supersedes the
   intermediate drawn-cash proposal. Do not require current drawn cash or a
   capital-call schedule for either initial or follow-on investments.
5. Expected reserve ratio is calculated from granular allocation assumptions,
   never entered as a target that changes the calculation.
6. Unfinanceable demand must not be labeled funded. A scenario may remain useful
   as an infeasible comparison with clear diagnostics.
7. Follow-on policy is user-provided. Fixed checks and pro-rata checks belong in
   this increment and combine with fund-config stage valuations, round sizes and
   option dilution. This supersedes the earlier fixed-dollar-only proposal.
8. Provide editable industry-median defaults where a comparable published median
   exists. Distinguish those from user assumptions and illustrative values;
   preserve the benchmark's population, period, statistic and units.
9. Aggregate preference-adjusted performance forecasts are an informational
   companion. Manual ownership and FMV overrides are valid when complex terms
   make simplified calculations unreliable. The companion cannot repair or
   invalidate construction feasibility or provide a fee-basis valuation path.

validation. Saved payloads contain the normalized fund facts, selected source

  The wizard displays millions while persistence forwards raw values and another
  contract documents dollars. Magnitude is not provenance. Ambiguous units
  refuse; do not silently multiply or relabel them.
- Require vintageYear, fund term, investment period and supported fee schedule.
  No current-year, zero-expense, zero-fee or default-tier fallback.
- Missing expenses differ from an explicit empty expense schedule. Support
  one-time organizational costs and recurring costs through explicit existing
  monthly windows, with start/end inclusivity documented and tested.
- Fee source precedence remains nonempty economicsAssumptions.feeModel.tiers,

  generalize monthly holiday support into support for arbitrary midyear tier

- Preserve the non-authoritative status of economicsAssumptions-derived facts.

Fee-basis refusal is a typed FEE_BASIS_UNSUPPORTED result with the actual basis,
source field path and one of the following reasons. Unknown enum values and

The invested-capital dependency is not an impossibility result. For an affine
case with per-company investment E and invested-basis fee K, expected count can
be solved as n=(commitments-fixed costs)/(E+K). An explicit budget or fixed
count also removes that particular sizing feedback. Those are different
adapters, not part of this increment; an override still does not waive the
accepted supported- fee requirement. Unrealized cost need not equal fair value
or require a returns engine, but assuming no dispositions to equate it with
invested cost is a new method choice. Never use the fee helper's
missing-schedule zero fallbacks to make any unsupported basis appear valid.

## Calculation contract: capital-planning/1.0.0

Money is USD fixed decimal strings; ratios have explicit semantics and range
checks. Use Decimal arithmetic and signed fixed-dp deltas. Years/months are
bounded integers, and arrays and decimal input lengths have finite contract
limits established before runtime wiring. Five variants alone is not a bound on
synchronous work.

Fund facts include committedCapitalUsd, vintageYear, investmentPeriodYears,
fundTermYears, validated managementFeeTiers and fundExpenses, source identity
and unit provenance. There is no credit-facility input.

Scenario assumptions include an optional explicit netInvestableCapitalUsd. The
fixed funding assumption is callable_as_needed; it is part of the saved
method/support metadata, not a switch to an operational liquidity model. V1 has
no recyclingAssumptionUsd input: reject that field in capital-plan payloads
rather than accept a financial amount with no calculation effect. Source
recycling fields are not materialized into this input. A user may record an
unmodeled possibility in the existing optional scenario description. Other
scenario kinds keep their existing contracts. No assumed proceeds, credit
facility or recycling allowance enlarges this increment's lifetime budget.

Each allocation includes allocationId, name, entryRound, source profile/stage
selection, budgetShareRatio, initialCheckUsd, deploymentPeriodYears, optional
plannedCompanyCount, and an ordered followOnRounds array. Every allocation
requires a positive initial check, nonnegative allocation share, and a
nonnegative integer planned count when supplied. Fixed follow-on checks are
positive; zero participation represents skipping them.

Each follow-on round includes a stable round/stage identity, roundLabel,
graduationRatio, participationRatio, checkPolicy and monthsAfterPreviousRound.
checkPolicy is a discriminated fixed_check (checkUsd) or pro_rata
(proRataExerciseRatio) value. Do not accept unspecified policies through free
text. Partial exercise is supported; additional policy types require a defined
calculation contract rather than interpretation of the word "etc."

For any allocation containing pro-rata follow-ons, include financing assumptions
at entry and every intervening modeled round, including rounds the fund skips:
valuationUsd with explicit pre_money/post_money basis, totalPrimaryRoundUsd
(including the fund's check), and incrementalPreMoneyPoolDilutionRatio at later
rounds. Entry uses an already fully diluted valuation and requires no additional
pool increment because the fund has no prior holding. Monetary values are
normalized to USD once. Positive pre-money and round size are required;
post-money equals pre-money plus primary round size. Initial check must be
positive and no larger than the entry round. Fixed checks cannot exceed a
supplied round size. Pool dilution is in [0,1); graduation, participation and
exercise are in [0,1]. Initial/follow-on shares, counts and budget ranges are
validated directly.

Fixed-only allocations can calculate capital demand without ownership inputs;
their ownership output is not modeled. Optional valid financing assumptions may
enable the same ownership explanation. A supplied ambiguous valuation or esopPct
must not be guessed or silently interpreted as incremental dilution.

### Policy check and ownership calculation

Use a simple primary equity-round convention: valuation is fully diluted after
the pre-money pool expansion, and round size includes the fund's contribution.
An incremental pool dilution d multiplies existing ownership by (1-d). It is
neither the final pool percentage nor new option shares divided by old shares.
Post-money pool changes and complex security conversions are unsupported until
an explicit adapter exists; show a typed field-level refusal for those inputs.

Entry ownership o_0 = initialCheck / (entryPreMoney + entryRoundSize). No
previous fund holding exists at entry to dilute a second time. For a later round
with conditional mean ownership o, normalized pre-money V, total primary round
R, participation p and pool dilution d:

- u = o*(1-d), the ownership after pool expansion and before new capital.
- Fixed policy: c = checkUsd per participating company.
- Pro-rata policy: c = proRataExerciseRatio*u*R, the conditional mean check
  among participating companies; individual checks differ with retained
  ownership.
- Participating post-round ownership = (u*V+c)/(V+R).
- Skipped post-round ownership = u*V/(V+R).
- Conditional mean post-round ownership = (u*V+p*c)/(V+R).

Full pro-rata preserves u against new financing dilution; it does not undo the
pool expansion. Do not label it as maintaining ownership before the pool change.
Carrying ownership forward through skipped rounds is mandatory. Multiplying
pre-round ownership by financing dilution before sizing a full pro-rata check
underfunds the policy; the report's shortcut is rejected.

This bounded mean recurrence assumes graduation and participation do not select
by prior ownership history within the homogeneous allocation/profile. Members
share the round's valuation, round size and dilution assumptions. Exactness also
requires affine check and ownership updates in prior ownership: expectation
commutes with these updates under shared coefficients and the selection
assumptions. Fixed checks add a constant; pro-rata checks are proportional to
ownership. Homogeneity alone would not make a nonlinear policy exact. Disclose
these assumptions with the result.

The construction recurrence refuses per-company check caps, minimum checks and
ownership-triggered rights with POLICY_UNSUPPORTED; it never inserts min/max or
a threshold into the mean recurrence. Future support requires an exactness
argument or additional state/distribution handling and a deliberate
method-version change. The existing check <= round-size validation is not a
nonlinear investment policy: it rejects invalid fixed inputs, and cannot bind on
valid pro-rata paths where exercise and ownership are both at most one. This
restriction concerns nonlinear construction policies; it does not exclude
participation caps in deterministic aggregate performance cases.

Selection-dependent policies require explicitly separate homogeneous
allocations; do not silently average incompatible profile inputs. There is no
random simulation, percentile or per-company cap-table engine. Graduation scales
expected counts, not ownership conditional on reaching the round. Zero exercise
generates no check or funded-company count.

Definitions:

- A = committed capital minus supported lifetime management fees and expenses. A
  is available construction capital under the callable-as-needed assumption, not
  evidence of present cash. GP commitments already included in total commitments
  are not deducted again as though they were an expense.
- N = explicit planning budget when supplied, otherwise A. Show the derived
  value and override difference separately. N cannot enlarge supported lifetime
  capital. Validate N as nonnegative; if A is negative, show costs exceeding
  commitments and no feasible construction instead of negative company counts.
- B_a = N times allocation share. Shares above one refuse; shares below one
  produce an explicitly unassigned budget.
- q_r = product of conditional graduation ratios through round r.
- participationRatio is the fraction of eligible graduated companies selected
  for the policy check. proRataExerciseRatio controls the fraction of rights
  exercised for a selected company. Neither substitutes for the other.
- check_r is the conditional mean check among selected eligible companies,
  calculated by the policy recurrence above; fixed-only policies supply it
  directly.
- E_a = initial check + sum(q_r times participation_r times check_r).
- n_a = B_a / E_a, retained fractionally. Expected round graduations are
  n_a*q_r; expected positive-check policy participation is
  n_a*q_r*participation_r when check_r is positive, otherwise zero. Label
  ownership as a conditional mean.
- Expected initial capital I_a = n_a*initialCheck. Expected follow-on demand F_a
  = sum(n_a*q_r*participation_r*check_r).
- Expected reserve ratio = F_a/(I_a+F_a), with its denominator labeled allocated
  investment capital. For a zero-budget allocation return null with a typed
  not-applicable reason rather than dividing by zero.
- An expected count is not an observed company count or an executable integer
  portfolio. Keep these labels in summaries and copied text.

Graduation means reaching the next round, conditional on reaching the previous
one, over the stated assumption context. Participation does not change
graduation. A skipped investment does not by itself prevent eligibility for a
later round. Do not introduce a separate exit multiplier, infer failure from
1-graduation, or force exit/graduation/failure to sum to one in this model.

Calculate each allocation/profile chain separately, then sum money and counts.
Do not multiply average transition rates across heterogeneous profiles. If
graduation and check size are selection-dependent, the check input describes the
selected graduated population; it is not an unconditional company average.

For plannedCompanyCount k, show k*initialCheck, k*(E_a-initialCheck), k*E_a and
B_a-k*E_a. Negative residual is an explicit over-budget diagnostic. This already
answers capital required for an entered count; no fund-size optimization or
reverse gross-commitment solver is added.

## Timing, conservation and financeability

Use regular monthly entry cohorts across deploymentPeriodYears*12 months. The
annual display aggregates those entry months; it does not round an 18-month
round interval into one or two years. Keep allocation, entry month and round
identity in the calculation breakdown.

For entry month t, round r occurs at t + sum(monthsAfterPreviousRound_1..r).
In-term months are 0 through fundTermYears*12-1. Demand at or after the term
boundary is beyond-term demand. It stays visible and is not relabeled funded
inside the term. Explicit initial deployment must fit the investment period.

Keep expected demand, designated follow-on reserves, unassigned construction
capital, in-term/beyond-term demand and lifetime budget headroom distinct.
Fractional expected demand is a planning quantity; a feasible expected schedule
is not a guarantee about every realized portfolio or today's cash balance.

### Lifetime feasibility and designated reserves

Calculate available construction capital from commitments after protecting the
complete lifetime fee/expense budget once. Assume capital can be drawn whenever
scheduled modeled investments require it. Do not gate initial or follow-on
demand on current cash, observed calls, a receipt calendar or settlement
completion. There is no requirement to draw the full fund at t=0. The annual
schedule reports expected capital demand, not observed cash movements or
executed capital calls.

For each allocation, reserve its derived lifetime follow-on demand F_a for the
follow-ons it protects. The initial-investment budget is B_a-F_a. Initials
cannot consume that earmark; eligible follow-ons can. Consuming a designated
follow-on reduces both remaining allocation capital and its remaining reserve
once. Do not subtract the reserve from the funding available to its own intended
follow-on. These are budget earmarks, not funded bank balances or a
cash-transfer schedule.

Expected sizing n_a=B_a/E_a assigns I_a+F_a=B_a. A fixed-count case k and named
stresses retain their count basis and report initial/follow-on demand against
allocation budgets, designated reserves and the fund ceiling A. A reserve gap
can exist while unassigned fund capacity remains; report both rather than
assuming an automatic transfer or changing the strategy. No allocation order
selects winners, reduced checks or deferred rounds when the candidate exceeds
its budget. An explicit budget override cannot increase A, and fees are not
deducted again when evaluating investment uses against the already net budget.

Round timing is independent of capital availability. Regular initial pacing and
cumulative monthsAfterPreviousRound operate together. A funding shortfall does
not shift modeled financing dates. Identify affected allocations/rounds and the
first demand month where a relevant budget limit is crossed, but label it a
construction-budget shortfall, never a current-liquidity or settlement failure.
Demand beyond term remains visible without becoming an in-term investment.

The earlier repository cash audit is retained as context for a separate
operational forecast. Its observed-cash rule, capital-call policy helpers and
quarterly called-capital fee input are not F1.15 calculation dependencies. A
separate operational model would require observed cash, call timing, settlement,
protected funded reserves and any eligible recycling receipts.

Required disclosure, verbatim in schedule, comparison and copied output:

> This schedule assumes capital can be called as needed. It does not model
> current cash availability or capital-call execution.

Keep the required timing disclosure unchanged. Also show this budget-scope
statement beside each feasibility verdict and in the copied output:

> Available construction capital excludes recycling and exit proceeds; fee
> offsets are not modeled. Feasibility is evaluated under these assumptions.

Label an over-budget result "Infeasible under modeled assumptions". It is not a
claim that the actual fund has no possible capital-reuse alternative. No offset,
recycling or forecast proceeds are credited to the construction calculation. The
informational performance companion below does not change this boundary.

Exact conceptual identities before output quantization:

- F_lifetime = F_within_term + F_beyond_term.
- I + F_lifetime + unassigned planning budget = N.
- The in-term schedule sums to I + F_within_term.

Retain adequate Decimal working precision for accepted input bounds. Quantize
emitted monetary leaves once and construct displayed aggregates from those
leaves. Include a named signed rounding residual explicitly in the
reconciliation identity. Do not add beyond-term demand a second time or hide
unexplained drift. Allocation ordering cannot select who receives rounding
residue.

"No left-over capital" means the assigned continuous budget is used in
expectation. Reserved future spending is assigned capital. Intentional
unassigned shares, integer-count residuals, rounding residual and infeasible
demand remain visible; none justifies forcing an investment.

## Informational aggregate performance companion

Keep one optional performanceCase per capital_plan variant, using existing
variant comparison for alternatives. Each case represents one issuer/holding or
an explicitly labeled representative issuer, not a combined preference stack for
unrelated companies. This is a deterministic what-if, not a probability-weighted
expected portfolio return. Evaluating a nonlinear preference formula at
conditional mean ownership does not establish expected proceeds; any copied mean
must retain the representative-case label.

The companion is a separate pure calculation, versioned
aggregate-preference-forecast/1.0.0. Construction assumptions may prefill it
only as explicit saved copies. Changing, adding or removing a performance case
must leave construction budgets, counts, reserves, demand schedule, stresses and
feasibility unchanged. Exit dates do not terminate graduation chains, fund
follow-ons, release earmarks or create capital-call/recycling events.

### Aggregate preference method

This method fixes other holders' entered preference claims and uses f as a fixed
share of residual common proceeds. Other holders' conversion elections,
participation terms and common ownership are unknown; no denominator
renormalization or simultaneous class elections are inferred. Senior/pari/junior
refer to the preference route. On the fund's conversion route, all remaining
preferences precede its common recovery, including J. State these assumptions
beside the case. They are deliberate approximations, not cap-table identities.

Let E be exit equity value. Evaluate mutually exclusive routes:

1. Preference route: seniorPaid=min(E,S); remaining=max(0,E-S). Pay the pari
   layer pariPaid=min(remaining,P+Q). Fund preference recovery
   L=pariPaid*P/(P+Q), or zero when P+Q=0; the other pari recovery is
   pariPaid-L. Pay juniors juniorPaid=min(remaining-pariPaid,J). Residual common
   pool H=remaining-pariPaid-juniorPaid=max(0,E-S-P-Q-J).
2. Non-participating preferred candidate V=L. Participating preferred candidate
   V=L+f*H; if capped, V=min(K,L+f*H). Any cap-limited excess remains outside
   the fund's recovery; it is not construction capital.
3. Assume voluntary full conversion is available. Common-conversion candidate
   C=f*max(0,E-S-Q-J), removing only the fund's P from the stack. Other claims
   remain fixed. Compare both routes, including voluntary conversion above a
   participation cap: adjusted proceeds=max(V,C). Report preference, conversion
   or indifferent as the selected route. Never add the preferred recovery to the
   selected conversion recovery.

Cooley GO's preferred-stock glossary supports the general distinction between
non-participating election and participating recovery. The fixed-other-claims
and fixed-residual-share formulas here are this plan's explicit approximation;
neither Cooley nor the Tactyc attachment is evidence for those exact formulas.
Reference: `https://www.cooleygo.com/glossary/preferred-stock/`.

### Outputs, breakpoints and disclosure

Display the entered exit date/value, effective ownership and overrides, claim
amounts, chosen route and the two route candidates, followed by:

- No-preference baseline N0=f*E, ignoring all preferences. It is different from
  C, which still honors other holders' claims.
- Preference-adjusted proceeds max(V,C), approximate gross holding-level MOIC
  max(V,C)/D, and baseline MOIC N0/D. D=0 yields null MOIC with ZERO_COST, never
  infinity, a fabricated denominator or a replacement by FMV.
- Incremental preference benefit=max(V,C)-N0, a signed amount labeled "versus
  no-preference baseline." It can be negative because of other holders' claims.
  The fund's own election uplift max(V,C)-C cannot be negative; do not confuse
  these comparison bases or clamp the signed baseline delta.
- Effective FMV and its separate as-of date/basis when provided. FMV is not an
  exit payment or a MOIC input. An exit date beyond the fund term is an
  informational notice, never a feasibility change or date repair.

For f>0, write B=S+Q+J. Relevant exit-equity-value breakpoints are:

| Case                                    | Breakpoint                                                                                                               |
| --------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Non-participating; or participating K=P | Conversion catches preference at B+P/f, including B when P=0.                                                            |
| Participating K>P                       | Total payout cap first reached at B+P+(K-P)/f; conversion catches the cap at B+K/f.                                      |
| Participating K=P>0                     | Cap first reached at S+P+Q, before junior claims necessarily finish. Payout/election otherwise equals non-participating. |
| Participating P=K=0                     | Cap already reached at E=0; conversion strictly dominates above B. No own preference protection exists.                  |

Equality is indifferent. At f=1, the K>P cap and conversion breakpoints
coincide; no positive-width capped plateau is implied. Uncapped participation
has no cap or conversion crossover (f=1 can tie after preferences are paid).
Conversion thresholds mark where conversion starts to strictly exceed the
preference route, not the first equality: zero-recovery ties can occur earlier.
When f=0, conversion breakpoints are null with ZERO_OWNERSHIP; a K=P cap is
still reached at S+P+Q when P>0 (at zero when P=0), whereas K>P cannot be
reached. When P=0, use the same route formulas: conversion weakly dominates any
capped participating route. For P=0,K>0,f>0, cap attainment and
strict-conversion thresholds both equal B+K/f; these route thresholds do not
imply a preference benefit. Uncapped participation with P=0 ties conversion
everywhere. Inapplicable breakpoints return a reason such as UNCAPPED,
ZERO_OWNERSHIP or NOT_PARTICIPATING, not infinity/NaN or a fabricated exit date.
Breakpoints describe value, not a time forecast.

Use Decimal working precision and the existing quantize-once convention. Trace
each route separately so allocated proceeds reconcile to E, and show only the
selected route as the fund payout. Assert 0<=adjusted proceeds<=E. A breakpoint
can be rounded for display; choose the route using full-precision candidates,
not the rounded breakpoint.

Required label, verbatim on companion results, comparisons and copied output:

> Aggregate preference forecast based on entered ownership and summarized
> preference terms. It does not reproduce a security-level cap table or legal
> distribution waterfall.

Also show that forecast proceeds do not fund construction. Preserve the
callable-as-needed and construction-budget disclosures wherever their existing
schedule/verdict rules apply. Do not describe these cases as legal distribution
instructions, automated valuation recommendations or security-level precision.
